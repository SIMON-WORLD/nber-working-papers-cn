Sticky Wage Norms and the Real Wage Cost of Unexpected Inflation

粘性工资规范与意外通胀的实际工资成本

Erik Hurst, Christina Patterson, Nela Thomas Richardson, Ye Liv Wang

宏观与货币财政 比利时、美国

Abstract

We use a sample of administrative payroll data covering a large and nationally representative share of U.S. workers to study how wages adjusted during the recent inflation period. Most firms apply a single modal annual nominal wage increase to the majority of their workers, and these firm-level norms changed little during the recent period of unexpected inflation. As a result, nominal wages did not keep pace with prices for a large share of workers who stayed at their firms. Forty-three percent of workers continuously employed at the same firm over the four years spanning 2021–2024 experienced a real wage decline, with a mean loss of roughly nine percent among those who fell behind. Workers could escape sticky wage norms by changing employers — job-changers’ wages rose nearly one-for-one with inflation — but switching was too infrequent to matter for most. Even accounting for job-changers, 37 percent of all workers saw real wages decline over the 4-year period. Indexing firms’ modal raises one-for-one to inflation would have closed roughly 40 percent of the resulting shortfall relative to prepandemic trend. Drawing on cross-country evidence from Belgium, whose wages are automatically indexed to inflation, we show that incomplete wage indexation, rather than inflation itself, helps explain the persistence of depressed consumer sentiment during the 2021–2024 period.

中文摘要

我们使用覆盖美国工人中具有全国代表性的大型行政工资单数据样本，研究在近期通胀期间工资是如何调整的。大多数公司对其大多数工人采用单一的年度名义工资增长模式，而这些公司层面的规范在近期意外通胀期间变化甚微。因此，对于留在原公司的很大一部分工人来说，名义工资未能跟上物价上涨。在2021年至2024年这四年间持续受雇于同一家公司的工人中，有43%经历了实际工资下降，其中落后者的平均损失约为9%。工人可以通过更换雇主来逃避粘性工资规范——换工作者的工资几乎与通胀率一比一上涨——但换工作的频率太低，对大多数人来说无关紧要。即使考虑到换工作者，仍有37%的所有工人在四年期间实际工资下降。将公司的模式化加薪与通胀率进行一比一的指数化，将弥补与疫情前趋势相比约40%的缺口。借鉴比利时的跨国证据（其工资自动与通胀挂钩），我们表明，不完全的工资指数化，而非通胀本身，有助于解释2021-2024年期间消费者情绪持续低迷的现象。
