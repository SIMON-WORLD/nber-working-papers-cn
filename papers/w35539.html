The China Backlash: Quantifying the Narrative Discourse on China

中国反弹：量化关于中国的叙事话语

Mert Geyiktepe, Dani Rodrik

教育与劳动 中国相关 美国

Abstract

Using text-as-data methods, we quantify the U.S. discourse on China, place it in historical perspective, and compare its evolution across different sources of public narrative. Our analysis is based on a large corpus of materials drawn from public records (presidential and congressional records, newspaper articles, social media, think tank reports). We develop two measures of narrative discourse: an indicator of the frequency with which China is covered and a proxy for how positively or negatively China is presented. We document a sustained decline in net sentiment towards China in presidential sources since the early 2000s, predating Donald Trump’s first term in office. In contrast to presidential documents, congressional, news media, social media, and think tank sources exhibit persistent negative sentiment towards China even in decades prior to the 2000s. Instead of a deterioration in sentiment across the board, what we find is a convergence in presidential sentiment to other sources of narrative discourse. We find no evidence that presidential leadership has played a significant role in setting the narrative tone for the nation as a whole. Historical and shorter-term evidence both point to a bottom-up process of diffusion of narratives, rather than top-down diffusion.

中文摘要

使用文本即数据方法，我们量化了美国关于中国的话语，将其置于历史视角中，并比较了不同公共叙事来源的演变。我们的分析基于从公共记录（总统和国会记录、报纸文章、社交媒体、智库报告）中提取的大型语料库。我们开发了两种叙事话语度量：中国被报道频率的指标，以及中国被呈现为积极或消极程度的代理指标。我们记录了自2000年代初以来总统来源中对中国的净情绪持续下降，这早于唐纳德·特朗普的第一个任期。与总统文件相比，国会、新闻媒体、社交媒体和智库来源在2000年代之前的几十年中就对中国的消极情绪持续存在。我们发现的不是情绪全面恶化，而是总统情绪向其他叙事话语来源的趋同。我们没有发现总统领导力在为国家整体设定叙事基调方面发挥重要作用的证据。历史证据和短期证据都指向叙事自下而上的扩散过程，而非自上而下的扩散。
