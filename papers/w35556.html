Meritocracy and the Networks It Forged: China’s Imperial Examination

贤能政治及其缔造的网络：中国的科举制度

Ying Bai, Ruixue Jia

其他主题 中国相关

Abstract

Institutions that claim to reward merit often also serve as engines that drive the formation of important social networks. We examine this dual role in China’s Imperial Examination, one of history’s longest-lived meritocratic systems. We draw on exam performance data for 24,269 individuals across 112 national examinations in the Qing dynasty (1644–1905) and trace who among them rose to become top officials. Strong exam performance increased the likelihood of attaining high office, but advancement also depended on individuals’ ties to powerful examiners. Because performance and ties to powerful examiners were strongly complementary, individuals’ success appeared to rest on merit even as social networks played a role. Following the major political transformations of the 1840s, the importance of examiner ties persisted, although the influential examiners themselves changed. These findings help explain why such systems retain legitimacy even when networks matter greatly.

中文摘要

声称奖励贤能的制度往往也充当推动重要社会网络形成的引擎。我们考察了科举制度——历史上寿命最长的贤能政治体系之一——中的这一双重角色。我们利用清朝（1644–1905年）112场全国性考试中24,269名考生的考试成绩数据，追踪其中哪些人晋升为高级官员。优异的考试成绩增加了获得高官职位的可能性，但晋升也取决于个人与有权势考官的联系。由于考试成绩与有权势考官的联系具有强烈的互补性，个人的成功看似基于贤能，即便社会网络也发挥了作用。在1840年代的重大政治变革之后，考官联系的重要性依然存在，尽管有影响力的考官本身发生了变化。这些发现有助于解释为何此类体系即使网络关系至关重要时仍能保持其合法性。
