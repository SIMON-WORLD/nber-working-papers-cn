The Distribution and Consequences of Natural Disasters: Evidence from U.S. Wildfire Victims

自然灾害的分布与后果：来自美国野火受害者的证据

Patrick Baylis, Judson Boomhower, Jonathan M. Colmer, John L. Voorheis

环境与气候 美国

Abstract

The economic burden of natural disasters is shaped by who is exposed, who experiences losses, how those losses alter individual economic trajectories, and how affected communities evolve in the aftermath. We link property-level damage data from over 100 U.S. wildfires to administrative tax and Census data to understand individual-level relationships between disaster damage and economic outcomes. Residents of burned areas are wealthier than the U.S. population on average, but within these wildfire perimeters the likelihood that a home burns is negatively correlated with pre-fire income. Home destruction imposes costs beyond rebuilding the property: occupants of destroyed homes experience lower income for three years post-fire, producing cumulative income losses equal to 26 percent of pre-fire annual income. We do not detect income or employment effects for residents without direct property damage. Community recovery is slow and accompanied by changes in composition. Four years after a fire, half of destroyed addresses remain unoccupied and reoccupied addresses house residents with substantially higher incomes than before.

中文摘要

自然灾害的经济负担取决于谁暴露于风险、谁遭受损失、这些损失如何改变个人经济轨迹，以及受灾社区在灾后如何演变。我们将来自100多起美国野火的财产级损失数据与行政税收和人口普查数据联系起来，以理解灾害损失与经济结果之间的个人层面关系。被烧毁地区的居民平均比美国人口更富有，但在这些野火边界内，房屋被烧毁的可能性与火灾前收入呈负相关。房屋毁坏带来的成本超出了重建财产本身：被毁房屋的居住者在火灾后三年内收入较低，导致累计收入损失相当于火灾前年收入的26%。我们没有检测到没有直接财产损失的居民的收入或就业效应。社区恢复缓慢，并伴随着人口构成的变化。火灾四年后，一半被毁地址仍无人居住，而重新居住的地址所容纳的居民收入远高于之前。
