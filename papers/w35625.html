When Public Signals Backfire: Strategic Disclosure and Information Crowd-Out

当公共信号适得其反：策略性披露与信息挤出

Marty Haoyuan Chen, Ginger Zhe Jin

教育与劳动 未指明区域

Abstract

We study voluntary disclosure when partially naive receivers observe a public signal correlated with the sender’s private information. We develop a framework in which the public signal changes how receivers interpret silence, and derive a closed-form disclosure threshold nesting classical unraveling. A laboratory experiment varying the correlation confirms our predictions: disclosure decreases as the signal becomes more favorable, and receiver guesses upon nondisclosure rise toward it. The public signal can crowd out disclosure, leaving receivers less informed than without it. This informational loss peaks at intermediate correlation, where the signal shifts beliefs yet remains too noisy to substitute for disclosure.

中文摘要

我们研究当部分天真的接收者观察到与发送者私人信息相关的公共信号时的自愿披露。我们开发了一个框架，其中公共信号改变了接收者如何解读沉默，并推导出一个嵌套经典“拆解”结果的闭式披露阈值。一个改变相关性的实验室实验证实了我们的预测：随着信号变得更加有利，披露减少，并且接收者在未披露时的猜测向其靠拢。公共信号可以挤出披露，使接收者比没有该信号时信息更少。这种信息损失在中等相关性时达到峰值，此时信号改变了信念，但仍过于嘈杂而无法替代披露。
