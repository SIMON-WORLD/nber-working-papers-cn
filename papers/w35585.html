Cycles of Infertility: Intergenerational Transmission and the Role of Assisted Reproduction

不孕的循环：代际传递与辅助生殖的作用

Viktoria Falk, Andreas Madestam, Emilia Simeonova

健康与医疗 瑞典

Abstract

This study examines the persistence of fertility-related health problems across generations within the same family, and the utilization of assisted reproductive technologies (ART). Using comprehensive Swedish registry data, we document significant intergenerational transmission of fertility difficulties for both daughters and sons, independent of parental socioeconomic status. Daughters whose parents reported such problems are 2.6 percentage points, or 19.4 percent, more likely to experience fertility problems themselves. Sons whose parents reported fertility difficulties experience a 1.7 percentage point, or 13.5 percent, increase. These associations persist across alternative measures and samples. Comparing biological and adopted children raised in the same households, we find that this phenomenon is unlikely to be driven by family awareness of fertility issues, pointing to biological and other factors that biological children share with their parents but adopted children do not. Medical technology works equally well for those who use it, and we find no significant association between the success of advanced fertility treatments and parental backgrounds. However, past generations’ fertility difficulties are associated both with higher medical expenses and with social costs such as increased likelihood of divorce.

中文摘要

本研究考察了同一家庭内生育相关健康问题的代际持续性，以及辅助生殖技术（ART）的使用情况。利用瑞典全面的登记数据，我们记录了女儿和儿子生育困难的显著代际传递，且与父母的社会经济地位无关。父母报告有此类问题的女儿，其自身经历生育问题的可能性高出2.6个百分点，即19.4%。父母报告有生育困难的儿子，其经历生育问题的可能性增加1.7个百分点，即13.5%。这些关联在替代指标和样本中持续存在。比较在同一家庭中长大的亲生子女和收养子女，我们发现这种现象不太可能由家庭对生育问题的认知所驱动，这表明是亲生子女与父母共享而收养子女不共享的生物学因素和其他因素在起作用。医疗技术对使用者的效果同样良好，我们发现高级生育治疗的成功率与父母背景之间没有显著关联。然而，过去几代人的生育困难既与更高的医疗费用相关，也与离婚可能性增加等社会成本相关。
