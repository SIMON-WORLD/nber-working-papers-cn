Information and Macroeconomic Expectations: Global Evidence

信息与宏观经济预期：全球证据

Francesco D’Acunto, Michael Weber

宏观与货币财政 全球/多国

Abstract

Unique global data on 47,000 consumers across 47 countries representing 90% of world GDP reveal universal patterns in macroeconomic belief formation. Most consumers form beliefs based on local information sources (utility bills, shopping, and acquaintances) that provide signals not representative for the overall consumption bundle, so that seeking information leads to forming biased macroeconomic expectations. Distrust in governments and central banks, the producers of aggregate economic information, helps explain reliance on local sources and the lack of learning from aggregate realizations. Observed macroeconomic volatility fuels distrust. In the cross-section, we confirm that certain demographics correlate with expectation biases but mostly due to different sorting into information sources, rather than different processing of the same economic information. These globally established facts inform advances of theories of belief formation and the design of communication strategies as a macroeconomic policy tool.

中文摘要

覆盖47个国家、代表全球90% GDP的47,000名消费者的独特全球数据揭示了宏观经济信念形成的普遍模式。大多数消费者基于本地信息来源（水电账单、购物和熟人）形成信念，这些来源提供的信号并不能代表整体消费篮子，因此寻求信息会导致形成有偏的宏观经济预期。对作为总体经济信息生产者的政府和中央银行的不信任，有助于解释对本地来源的依赖以及缺乏从总体实现值中学习的情况。观察到的宏观经济波动加剧了不信任。在横截面分析中，我们确认某些人口统计特征与预期偏差相关，但这主要是由于不同人群选择不同的信息来源，而非对相同经济信息的处理方式不同。这些全球确立的事实为信念形成理论的进展以及作为宏观经济政策工具的沟通策略设计提供了信息。
